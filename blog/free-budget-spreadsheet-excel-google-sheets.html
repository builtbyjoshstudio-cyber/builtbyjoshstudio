Free Budget Spreadsheet for Excel and Google Sheets: Try Before You Buy

The Short Version

A real working budget spreadsheet you can use this month — not a demo, not a screenshot, not a trial. The Ultimate Budget Workbook Lite includes every month of the year plus a Control Panel, formatted and ready to go.

What a Free Budget Spreadsheet Should Actually Do

A free budget spreadsheet should let you track income and expenses for a full year without hitting a paywall, signing up for an email list, or losing the file when a trial expires. Most "free" budget templates fail at least one of those tests. They give you one month and lock the rest. They strip the formulas and call it a "starter." They watermark the file. Or they hand you a working spreadsheet in exchange for your email, which is its own kind of cost.

The Ultimate Budget Workbook Lite is built on a different principle: a free version should be a real tool, not a marketing wrapper. It includes the Read Me, the Formatting Instructions, the Control Panel, and every monthly tab from January through December. It's fully formatted, fully usable, and works the same way the paid version does. The locked tabs in the Lite are the advanced ones — yearly summaries, debt tracking, financial goals — and they're locked the way premium DLC is locked in a video game: visible inside the workbook so you can see what you'd be getting if you upgrade, but not artificially crippling the core experience.

The Lite version is available in both Excel and Google Sheets. The Excel file includes both a blank workbook and a sample-data workbook so you can see how it behaves once filled in. The Google Sheets version is delivered through a PDF with clickable copy links — one click and the template is in your Drive, ready to use.

What's Inside the Lite Workbook

Six things are unlocked and fully working in the Lite version:

  1. Read Me — A walkthrough of the workbook structure and how the tabs connect. Tells you what's on the Control Panel, what each monthly tab does, and where the formulas live.
  1. Formatting Instructions — Visual reference for the conditional formatting that color-codes overspending, under-spending, and category totals.
  1. Control Panel — The dashboard tab. Set your monthly income, your category budgets, and your starting balance. Everything else flows from here.
  1. January through December tabs — Twelve monthly tabs, each pre-formatted with income, expenses, category totals, and a running balance. Add transactions, the math handles itself.
  1. Free password protection — Standard across all Tynkr workbooks. Lets you unlock cells for customization without breaking the formulas.
  1. Quick Start Guide PDF — A two-page setup guide delivered with the file. Tells you exactly what to do in the first ten minutes.

The locked tabs in the Lite version include the Annual Summary, Debt Snowball, Net Worth Tracker, and Goals modules — the ones that turn a monthly budget into a yearly financial system. Those unlock when you upgrade to the full Ultimate Budget Workbook. There's a static upgrade code inside the Lite that takes 35% off the full version with no expiration.

Excel or Google Sheets — Which One You Should Pick

Both versions of the Lite workbook are functionally identical. The difference comes down to where you already work and what you want the spreadsheet to do.

Choose the Excel version if you want offline access, slightly more advanced conditional formatting, and the option to use it as a Microsoft 365 file with mobile sync. Excel handles large transaction histories better than Sheets once you cross 1,000+ entries per month, which matters if you're tracking aggressively.

Choose the Google Sheets version if you want to collaborate with a partner or spouse on the same budget, access it from any device without a Microsoft account, or use it as the central node in a Google Drive workflow. Sheets also handles automation tools like Zapier and Make better if you want to pipe transactions in from elsewhere later.

Most users pick one and stick with it. A few download both Lite versions to test which one fits their workflow, then upgrade only the one they actually used.

How the Lite-to-Full Upgrade Works

The Lite is meant to be useful on its own. If a monthly budget tracker is all you need, the Lite covers that indefinitely. If you find yourself wanting the yearly view, debt tracking, or goals modules after a month or two, the upgrade is simple:

  1. Open the Quick Start Guide PDF inside the Lite download.
  2. Find the upgrade code on the upgrade page.
  3. Apply it at checkout.
  4. Transfer your data once during setup (most users finish in 15–20 minutes).

The code doesn't expire. You can sit on the Lite for a year and the upgrade is still 35% off when you're ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Lite budget spreadsheet actually free?

On Etsy it's $1 because that's the lowest price Etsy allows. You can also download it free from the free templates & tools hub.

Does the Lite version include all twelve months?

Yes. January through December are all unlocked and fully working. The locked tabs are the yearly-summary and advanced-tracking modules, not the monthly ones.

Do I need Microsoft 365 to use the Excel version?

A standard installed copy of Excel works fine. Excel Online (the free web version) also works, with some minor conditional formatting limitations.

Can my partner and I share the Google Sheets version?

Yes. That's one of the main reasons to pick Sheets over Excel for shared finances. After you copy the template into your Drive, you can share it with edit access like any other Sheet.

What happens to my data if I upgrade?

You transfer it once during setup. The full version has the same monthly tab structure, so transferring is mostly copy-paste plus connecting the Control Panel to the new yearly modules. The Quick Start Guide walks through it.

Can I customize the categories?

Yes. The Lite version uses the standard Tynkr workbook password to unlock category cells for editing. The formulas are protected separately, so customizing categories doesn't break the math.
